The idea in one line

When someone asks for a loan, we predict the chance they won't pay it back, compare that with what the loan earns, and answer yes, a smaller loan, or no, with the reasons in plain words.

How we think about it: five steps, in this order

1 · DecisionLend or not, and how much?Start from the business question, not the algorithm.
2 · PredictionChance she won't repayA percentage, not a yes/no, so the business chooses its own risk level.
3 · RuleCompare with the moneyIf the expected loss beats the expected fee, decline or lend less.
4 · ReasonsExplain every answerFeature by feature, weight by weight: enough to defend in court.
5 · LearnWatch and retrainTrack who repaid; update when customers change.

The model only does step 2. The Decision API is all five steps working together.

What the models learned from, and why each fact is there

A "feature" is one fact about a person that helps predict whether they repay. Picking features is most of the real thinking. A clever model on bad features is still a bad model.

The practice data

Home Credit lends to people with little or no credit history, the same kind of customer as M-KOPA or Aventus. In 2018 it published anonymised loans for a public competition; we used a free public copy.

  • loan applications, each marked "repaid" or "had trouble repaying" (about had trouble).
  • About 13 million past instalments: what was due, when, and what was paid. This gives each person's repayment habits.

Not Kenyan, not M-PESA, no names. Amounts are in the dataset's own currency. It proves the method; on Safaricom data the features come from Doris, built by the feature-engineering team.

How the 15 features were chosen

  1. Ask what a lender would ask: can they afford it, do they pay on time, are they stable, what do others say?
  2. Keep only facts Safaricom could supply with consent.
  3. Turn history into habits: 13M payments become "how often late", "how late", "how often short".
  4. Drop the unfair or invasive: gender (kept only to test fairness), friends' defaults, mostly-blank home details.
  5. Check direction: more late payments must never lower risk.

Slow lane: building a model

  1. Pull features from Doris (feature-engineering team's tables), consented customers only.
  2. Train v1 (logistic) and challengers (EBM, XGBoost) on older loans.
  3. Test on newer loans. Every gate below must pass.
  4. Register a version with a plain-English model card.
  5. Package as a container (Jenkins, one repo → one image) and run on Iguazio next to Doris.

Rolling out safely

  1. Shadow 2–4 weeks: score everything, decide nothing, compare.
  2. Small slice 5–10% of requests, compare repayment.
  3. Champion and challenger: a new model must win on profit to take over.
  4. Fallback: if slow or down, the tiny logistic model answers.

The blind spot

We only learn whether approved people repay. If the model wrongly rejects a group, we never find out, and the mistake locks itself in.

Fix: give a small, budgeted number of near-the-line applicants a starter loan anyway, and learn from them.

When we retrain

  • Applicants have drifted (stability index over 0.25)
  • Performance on recent loans drops
  • New product or policy
  • Otherwise every quarter
For engineers

Features computed daily by an MLRun job from Doris, cached in Redis for millisecond reads; one shared feature module for training and serving. FastAPI service as a stateless MLRun application (AD-3). Cut-offs in the partner's YAML. Async audit log of features, model version, score, decision, reasons. Reject inference via a randomised exploration band. PSI = Σ (a − e)·ln(a/e) over 10 score bands.
