Chapter 1 · Start here

When someone asks for a loan, we predict the chance they won't repay, then let the money decide.

The answer comes back in milliseconds as yes, a smaller loan, or no, with the reasons in plain words. That answer is the decision slip below. Everything in this workbook explains how one slip gets made.

Five steps, in this order

  1. The decision. Lend or not, and how much? We start from the business question, not from an algorithm.
  2. The prediction. A model gives one number: the chance she won't repay.
  3. The line. Compare that chance with what a loan earns and what a default costs.
  4. The reasons. Show which facts pushed the answer. A wrong refusal costs a good customer and the partner real money, so every answer must be explainable.
  5. The loop. Watch who actually repays, and retrain when customers change.

The model only does step 2. The Decision API is all five steps working together.

Chapter 2 · The data

A model is only as good as the facts it's given.

A "feature" is one fact about a person that helps predict whether they repay. Choosing them is most of the real thinking.

Sourcepractice data

Home Credit lends to people with little or no credit history, the same kind of customer as M-KOPA or Aventus. It published anonymised loans for a public competition in 2018. We used a free public copy: about 307,000 loan applications, each marked "repaid" or "had trouble repaying" (roughly 8 in 100 had trouble), plus about 13 million past instalment payments.

Not Kenyan, not M-PESA, no names. It proves the method. On real work the features come from Doris, built by the feature-engineering team.

Choosinghow features were picked
  1. Ask what a lender would ask. Can they afford it? Do they pay on time? Are they stable? What do others say?
  2. Keep only what Safaricom could supply with the customer's consent.
  3. Turn history into habits. 13 million payments become "how often late", "how late", "how often short".
  4. Drop the unfair or invasive. Gender is kept only to test fairness, never as an input.
  5. Check direction. More late payments must never lower risk.

AUCPick one person who didn't repay and one who did. AUC is the chance the model gave the non-payer the higher risk. A ranking score.0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. 0.70–0.80 is typical in credit.

Chapter 6 · Serving it

From a trained model to an answer in under a tenth of a second.

Two lanes. A slow lane builds and tests models in the background. A fast lane answers each loan request.

Fast laneone loan request

The partner sends:

POST /v1/decision
{ "partner": "lendplus",
  "customer_ref": "hashed-msisdn",
  "consent_id": "c-2026-11-0042",
  "amount_kes": 5000,
  "tenor_days": 30 }

And gets back:

{ "decision": "approve",
  "risk": 0.072,
  "max_amount_kes": 5000,
  "reasons": ["Pays instalments on time",
              "Regular monthly inflows",
              "SIM held over 2 years"],
  "model": "logreg-1.0.0" }
Slow lanebuilding a model
  1. Pull features from the feature team's Doris tables, consented customers only.
  2. Train v1 (logistic) and the challengers on older loans.
  3. Test on newer loans. Every test below must pass.
  4. Register the version with a plain-English model card.
  5. Package as a container and run it on Iguazio, next to Doris.

Rolloutgoing live safely
  1. Shadow, 2–4 weeks. The model scores every request but decides nothing. We compare.
  2. Small slice, 5–10%. It decides for a few customers. We compare repayment.
  3. Champion and challenger. A new model must make more money to take over.
  4. Fallback. If the service is slow or down, the tiny logistic model answers. A request never hangs.

Retrain when applicants have changed, when results on recent loans drop, when a partner changes product or policy, and otherwise every quarter.

The blind spot. We only learn whether approved people repay. If the model wrongly rejects a group, we never find out. The fix is to give a small, budgeted number of near-the-line applicants a starter loan anyway and learn from them.

For engineers

Features computed daily by an MLRun job from Doris and cached in Redis for millisecond reads; one shared feature module for training and serving. FastAPI service as a stateless MLRun application, one repo → one Jenkins image. Cut-offs live in the partner's YAML. Every request logged asynchronously with features, model version, score, decision and reasons. Drift measured with the population stability index: below 0.10 stable, above 0.25 act. Monitoring tables land in Doris and show in Superset.
